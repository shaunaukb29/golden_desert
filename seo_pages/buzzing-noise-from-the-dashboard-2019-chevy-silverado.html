Common Car Noises & What They Usually Mean

Not sure what to call the sound? Start here to diagnose car problems by sound, then confirm it with the diagnoser below.

Why is my car making a buzzing noise?

A buzzing noise often points to a loose heat shield, a failing accessory belt tensioner, a worn wheel bearing, or a vibration in the dashboard trim or exhaust system. Speed-dependent buzzing usually means a rotating part; buzzing that only happens at idle more often points to a belt, engine mount, or an electrical component like a relay or blower motor.

Why is my car making a rattling noise?

Rattling is one of the most common noise complaints and one of the widest in terms of possible causes — loose heat shields, worn suspension bushings, a failing catalytic converter, loose exhaust hangers, or something loose in the cabin or trunk. Rattling that changes with engine speed usually traces back to the engine or exhaust; rattling over bumps usually points to suspension or the underbody.

Why is my car making a humming noise?

A humming or droning noise that increases with speed is a classic sign of a worn wheel bearing or uneven tire wear. Humming from the drivetrain area can also point to a differential or transmission issue, especially if it changes under acceleration versus coasting.

Why is my car making a squeaking noise?

Squeaking is most often brakes — either wear indicators doing their job, or glazed pads. It can also come from a dry belt, a strut or suspension component, or a door/window seal. Squeaking that only happens when braking almost always means brakes; squeaking over bumps usually means suspension.

My car is making a ticking noise — why is my engine ticking?

A ticking noise from the engine bay is commonly low oil pressure, a lifter or valvetrain tick, or an exhaust leak near the manifold — these vary widely in urgency, from harmless to serious, which is exactly the kind of case worth cross-referencing sound against a fault code if you have one.

Car making noise when turning?

Noise on turns usually points to CV joints (a clicking or popping sound turning one direction), power steering (a whining sound tied to wheel angle), or wheel bearings (a growl that shifts side to side as you turn). Which direction it's worse in is a strong clue to which side is affected.

Diagnose this turning noise →

Why does my car squeal when I start it?

A squeal right at startup — especially in cold weather — usually means a glazed or slightly loose serpentine belt struggling to grip the pulleys until things warm up, or a failing tensioner or idler pulley. It's rarely urgent on its own, but a belt that's slipping enough to squeal is also a belt that's wearing faster than it should.

Why is my transmission making a whistling noise?

A whistling or whining noise tied to engine RPM rather than road speed often points to low or degraded transmission fluid, a failing pump, or early torque converter wear. Whistling that changes with road speed instead — even with the car in neutral — is more likely a wheel bearing, tire, or wind noise rather than the transmission itself.
