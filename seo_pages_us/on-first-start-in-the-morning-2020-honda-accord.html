Why Is My 2020 Honda Accord Making a Squealing Noise On First Start In The Morning?

⚠ LOW — monitor it; schedule a checkup 2020 Honda Accord · Updated August 2026
The Short Answer

A squealing noise on first start in the morning on a 2020 Honda Accord most commonly comes from glazed or slightly slipping serpentine belt, worn tensioner or idler pulley bearing, or belt contaminated with oil or coolant. Urgency: LOW — monitor it; schedule a checkup.

What mechanics check first: With the hood up, listen whether the squeal is coming from the front of the engine (accessory belt area) or from a wheel. That immediately splits it into belt vs. brake.

5 Most Likely Causes of squealing noise on first start in the morning on a 2020 Honda Accord

#1

Glazed or slightly slipping serpentine belt

A high-pitched squeal on cold startup that fades within 30–60 seconds is almost always a serpentine belt that's glazing on the pulleys until everything warms up and the belt grips properly. Belts typically last 60k–100k miles, but glazing can happen earlier in humid climates. Diagnose this exact noise →

#2

Worn tensioner or idler pulley bearing

If the squeal takes longer to go away, or comes back intermittently during the drive, the tensioner or idler pulley bearing may be failing. These pulleys spin constantly and wear out faster than the belt itself. A bad bearing squeals and can seize, breaking the belt. Diagnose this exact noise →

#3

Belt contaminated with oil or coolant

A belt that's been contaminated by an oil or coolant leak will squeal because the friction surface is compromised. If the squeal gets worse rather than better, or if you notice a burnt rubber smell, inspect for a leaking valve cover gasket or coolant hose nearby. Diagnose this exact noise →

#4

Cold-morning brake pad squeal

Brake pads can squeal on the first few stops of the morning from surface rust on the rotors (especially in humid states). This usually goes away after 2–3 stops. If it doesn't, the wear indicators may be making contact with the rotor. Diagnose this exact noise →

#5

Power steering pump cavitation (cold fluid)

On vehicles with hydraulic power steering, a brief squeal when first starting in cold weather — especially combined with stiff steering — can come from the power steering pump struggling to move cold, thick fluid. It usually quiets within a minute as the fluid warms. Diagnose this exact noise →

Common Questions About This Noise

Is it safe to drive my 2020 Honda Accord with this squealing noise?

In most cases you can drive short distances, but get it inspected soon — some causes of a squealing noise on first start in the morning can worsen quickly if left unaddressed. How much does it cost to fix a squealing noise on a 2020 Honda Accord?

Repair cost depends heavily on the root cause. A heat shield rattle can cost under $50. A wheel bearing runs $200–$450 including labor. A timing chain replacement on a Honda Accord can reach $1,000–$2,500. Can I diagnose this noise myself without a mechanic?

For many of the causes listed above, yes — things like heat shield rattles, loose end links, and glazed belts can be inspected at home. For anything involving the engine internals, bearings, or brakes, you'll want a hands-on confirmation. What if the noise only happens in cold weather?

Cold-weather-specific noises often point to lubricants that are too thick when cold (engine oil, differential fluid, transmission fluid), thermal contraction causing components to sit differently, or moisture-related issues like surface rust on brake rotors. Many of these are harmless and clear up as the vehicle warms.
