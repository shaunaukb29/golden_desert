Why Is My 2019 Ford Explorer Making a Clunking Noise When Braking?

⚠ HIGH — inspect before driving again 2019 Ford Explorer · Updated August 2026
The Short Answer

A clunking noise when braking on a 2019 Ford Explorer most commonly comes from loose brake caliper bolt, worn brake pads (metal-on-metal), or worn shock absorber mounts. Urgency: HIGH — inspect before driving again.

What mechanics check first: Try to reproduce the clunk with the window down at low speed in a parking lot. Note which corner it seems to come from — that narrows the caliper or pad inspection significantly.

5 Most Likely Causes of clunking noise when braking on a 2019 Ford Explorer

#1

Loose brake caliper bolt

A single loud clunk when you first apply the brakes — especially after the car has sat overnight — often means a loose or missing caliper bolt. The caliper shifts under braking load and clunks against the bracket. This is a safety issue that needs immediate attention. Diagnose this exact noise →

#2

Worn brake pads (metal-on-metal)

When brake pads wear past the friction material, the metal backing plate contacts the rotor. This creates a grinding or clunking sound under braking, especially on the first stop of the day. If you're hearing metal-on-metal, the rotor is being damaged every time you brake. Diagnose this exact noise →

#3

Worn shock absorber mounts

A clunk from the front when braking hard often traces to a worn strut mount or shock absorber upper mount. The nose-dive under braking shifts weight forward and the worn mount clunks. Common on high-mileage RAV4s, Explorers, and Accords. Diagnose this exact noise →

#4

Anti-rattle clips missing or broken

Brake pad anti-rattle clips hold the pads firmly in the caliper bracket. If they break or fall off — common during a cheap brake job — the pad shifts and clunks when you apply and release the brake pedal. Diagnose this exact noise →

#5

Loose lug nut or wheel bearing play

A clunking or knocking when braking that changes sides can sometimes trace to a loose lug nut or significant wheel bearing play. Under deceleration, the weight shifts and the loose component clunks. Always torque lug nuts to spec after any tire work. Diagnose this exact noise →

How much does it cost to fix a clunking noise on a 2019 Ford Explorer?

Repair cost depends heavily on the root cause. A heat shield rattle can cost under $50. A wheel bearing runs $200–$450 including labor. A timing chain replacement on a Ford Explorer can reach $1,000–$2,500. Can I diagnose this noise myself without a mechanic?

For many of the causes listed above, yes — things like heat shield rattles, loose end links, and glazed belts can be inspected at home. For anything involving the engine internals, bearings, or brakes, you'll want a hands-on confirmation. What if the noise only happens in cold weather?

Cold-weather-specific noises often point to lubricants that are too thick when cold (engine oil, differential fluid, transmission fluid), thermal contraction causing components to sit differently, or moisture-related issues like surface rust on brake rotors. Many of these are harmless and clear up as the vehicle warms.
