Buying a Used Car?
The Master Checklist

A no-fluff, engineering-grade inspection list covering exactly what you must check to avoid scams, lemons, and money pits.

By Carithm Team • Jul 28, 2025 • 6 Min Read

Buying a pre-owned vehicle can be an incredibly smart financial decision—especially with today’s drastically inflated new car prices. But it can rapidly deteriorate into a devastatingly costly mistake if you aren’t aggressively thorough.

Whether you are purchasing from a large dealership lot or negotiating with a private seller in a driveway, here is an actionable, no-nonsense checklist you must follow before signing any title paperwork. (If you hear or see something suspicious during your inspection, consult Carithm AI for an instant second opinion).

1 Vehicle History Report

Always commence your purchasing journey by legally requesting a vehicle history report. Utilizing services like Carfax or AutoCheck explicitly reveals if the vehicle has been involved in catastrophic structural accidents, suffered permanent flood damage, been subjected to illegal odometer rollbacks, or survived as an abused rental fleet or taxi vehicle. Never, under any circumstance, skip this preliminary step—it is your paramount line of defense.

2 Inspect for Rust or Body Damage

Conduct a meticulous walk-around of the automobile and surveil exterior panels closely for corrosive rust, mismatched paint hues (indicating post-crash resprays), or sloppy previous amateur repairs. While negligible surface scratches are expected, heavily bubbled paint, pervasive rust rot, or severely misaligned hood panels hint at deeper structural chassis trauma. Bring a powerful flashlight to inspect underneath heavy wheel wells and underbody subframes.

3 Check the Tires

Analyze the tires—they tell a story. Dramatically uneven tire wear almost certainly signals severely compromised suspension struts or catastrophic wheel alignment issues. All tires should wear perfectly evenly across the main tread and possess at least a minimum of 4/32” tread left. Being forced to replace all four expensive tires immediately after buying a car is an immediate hidden expense you want to rigorously avoid.

4 Examine the Engine Bay

Unlatch and lift the front hood. Scavenge visually for aggressive oil leaks, deeply cracked serpentine belts, or heavily corroded battery terminals. Curiously, if the used engine bay looks impossibly spotless and glossy, view it with skepticism—unscrupulous sellers often steam-clean engine bays specifically to eradicate the visual evidence of chronic oil or coolant leaks. Note any strange wiring splices.

5 Quality of Fluid Levels

Pull the engine dipsticks to check the oil, transmission fluid, brake reserves, and coolant tanks. Engine oil should be relatively clean and amber-brown—not pitch black, thick, and gritty. Automatic transmission fluid should optimally be cherry pink or red, never burnt brown or carrying a charred smell. Neglected, dirty fluids overwhelmingly correlate with extreme owner negligence and drastically shortened engine lifespans.

6 Rigorous Test Drive

Do not just circle the residential block below 30 mph. Force the vehicle into real-world, high-stress conditions: push highway speeds, execute sharp cornering, brake aggressively, and sit in prolonged idling. Listen vehemently for ticking or grinding noises, pay close attention to steering vibrations, and check if the car veers off alignment. If something feels mechanically disastrous, absolutely trust your gut.

7 Test All Interior Electronics

Methodically engage every single electronic switch in the cabin. Test the main radio, aggressively blast the A/C and the heater, operate all automatic windows, actuate the power locks, test blinkers/headlights, and navigate the entire digital infotainment system menu. Contemporary cars are practically rolling computers, and successfully replacing a shattered or bricked infotainment screen module can cost thousands out-of-pocket.

8 Scan for OBD-II Codes

Even if the orange Check Engine Light is presently unlit, hidden malfunction codes may still be permanently stored in the car's Engine Control Unit (ECU). Connect an inexpensive Bluetooth OBD-II scanner securely into the driver's under-dash port to interrogate the computer. If confusing codes emerge, feed them instantly into Carithm AI to decode what catastrophic failures you might be unwittingly inheriting.

9 Look at the Tailpipe Exhaust

Cold-start the car and meticulously observe the resulting exhaust emissions. The presence of heavily tinted smoke is a massive warning beacon:

  • Blue Smoke: The engine is internally burning oil.
  • White Smoke: Vaporized coolant is actively leaking directly into the combustion chamber (Blown Head Gasket).
  • Black Smoke: The engine is running rich and inefficiently dumping excess, unburned fuel.

10 Negotiate With Leverage

Leverage every single minor defect you discovered during the inspection checklist aggressively into financial negation. If the installed tires are bald or the brake rotors are severely warped, that translates directly into real currency shaved off the final asking price. And if a structural issue feels critically wrong, do not exhibit fear—walk away immediately. There is an endless supply of vehicles.
