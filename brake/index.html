What Brake Faults Can We Diagnose?

  • Wear Indicator Squeal: High-pitched, consistent squeal that appears whenever the brakes are used.
  • Metal-on-Metal Grinding: Harsh, grating scrape meaning the pads are fully worn through.
  • Warped Rotor Pulsation: Rhythmic vibration or noise tied to wheel speed, often with a pulsing pedal.
  • Glazed Pad Squeak: Higher-pitched, intermittent squeak from hardened, overheated pad material.
  • Caliper / Hardware Rattle: Loose clunking or rattling, often worse over bumps.
  • Surface Rust Squeal: Light morning squeal that clears up after the first few stops.

Brake Noises Explained

Not sure what your specific brake noise implies? Here are the most common symptoms searched by drivers. Match your situation, then upload a recording for an AI confidence score.

Why do my brakes squeal when I stop?

A consistent high-pitched squeal is most often the brake pad's built-in metal wear indicator lightly scraping the rotor. It's an intentional early-warning feature designed to tell you the pads are getting thin, not necessarily a sign of an emergency, but it does mean a pad replacement is coming up soon.

Why do my brakes grind when I stop?

Grinding is a harsher, deeper, metallic scraping sound. It usually means the pad material has completely worn away and the metal backing plate is now grinding directly against the rotor. This can damage the rotor quickly and reduces your stopping power, so it should be addressed right away.

Why do my brakes squeal only when it's cold or wet?

A light squeal in the early morning or after rain that disappears after a few stops is usually just surface rust or condensation on the rotor being cleared away by the pads as you drive. This is common and typically not a concern on its own.

Why do my brakes squeak only at low speed?

Squeaking that shows up mainly at low speed or during the first stops of a drive is frequently related to glazed pad material (hardened from overheating) or a light layer of brake dust on the rotor surface, rather than a sign the pads need immediate replacement.

Why do my brakes make noise only when turning?

Noise that only appears while turning, and not during straight-line braking, is often caused by a loose heat shield vibrating, a small rock or piece of debris trapped between the rotor and the shield, or uneven pad wear on one side of the vehicle.

My brakes are grinding but the pads still look fine — why?

If pad thickness looks acceptable but you're still hearing grinding, check for a corroded rotor edge (common on rear rotors that see less use), a seized caliper slide pin causing uneven contact, or debris trapped against the rotor. These can all produce a grinding sound independent of pad wear.

Is it safe to drive with squealing brakes?

A steady, high-pitched squeal from the wear indicator is generally safe to drive on for a short time while you schedule a pad replacement. However, you should not ignore it for long, since continued driving lets the pads wear down to metal-on-metal contact.

Is it safe to drive with grinding brakes?

No. Grinding almost always means reduced braking performance and active damage to your rotors. Continuing to drive on grinding brakes can lead to costly rotor replacement, caliper damage, and in severe cases a real loss of stopping power. Have it inspected immediately.
