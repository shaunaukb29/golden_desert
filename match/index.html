What This Actually Tells You — and What It Doesn't

Be honest with yourself about the limits here

A sound check is good at catching things that haven't announced themselves yet — early belt wear, a bearing starting to hum, brake pads getting thin. It can't tell you whether the car has been in an accident, whether the odometer has been rolled back, or whether it's been flood-damaged. Those need the document and walk-around checks below, done in person, every time.

The Walk-Around Checklist

Do this in daylight, before you get in for the test drive.

01
Match the Mulkiya to the carConfirm the chassis number on the registration card matches the number stamped on the vehicle, usually visible through the windshield or under the hood.
02
Check panel gaps and paint matchStand at the corner of the car and sight down each body line. Uneven gaps or paint that looks slightly off between adjacent panels in direct sun both point to prior accident repair.
03
Look under the hood for fresh bolts or oversprayBent brackets, replaced bolts with clean heads, or paint mist on a bracket that shouldn't be painted are signs of collision repair work.
04
Check tire wear and date codesUneven wear across the tread points to alignment or suspension issues. The date code on the sidewall tells you the tire's real age, regardless of how new it looks.
05
Feel the AC within the first minuteIn Gulf heat, a compressor under strain is one of the more common near-term costs on an older used car. It should blow genuinely cold quickly, not just cool.
06
Ask for the service book and inspection certificateA stamped service history and the most recent RTA or Ministry-mandated inspection (if the car is old enough to require one) are worth more than a verbal assurance.

Buying Through Dubizzle or OpenSooq? A Few Things First

Private-sale listings move fast, and sellers relocating or upgrading are often motivated to close quickly — which isn't a red flag by itself, but it does mean the burden of checking is on you, not a dealership's reputation.

  • Meet in daylight, not after dark, so you can actually see panel and paint condition.
  • Confirm the car is either fully owned or the outstanding loan will be cleared before transfer — an unresolved finance can complicate registration transfer later.
  • Ask directly whether the car has ever been in an accident. A truthful seller usually just tells you; a vague non-answer is itself information.
  • Take the test drive on both a stretch of highway and stop-start city driving — some noises and vibrations only show up at one or the other.

Why a Gulf-Registered Car Wears on a Different Schedule

Mileage tells you distance covered, not what the climate has done along the way. A car that's spent its life in sustained high heat puts more strain on specific components than the same car and mileage in a milder climate.

AC Compressor

Runs harder and more often through the year, which shortens its working life relative to the same part in a temperate climate.

Rubber Belts & Hoses

Heat cycling hardens and cracks rubber faster, which is why a belt squeal on a Gulf car can show up well before the mileage would suggest it.

Battery Life

High ambient heat is harder on battery chemistry than cold is — expect a shorter working life than the same battery would see elsewhere.

Coolant System

Radiators and hoses work under more sustained load, so it's worth asking when the coolant was last replaced, not just whether it looks full.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a professional pre-purchase inspection for a used car in the UAE?

For anything above a routine budget purchase, yes — a paid mechanical inspection is worth it, especially for cars over 100,000km. A sound check at the viewing is a fast way to decide whether a car is worth booking that inspection for in the first place, not a replacement for one.

Is a sound check enough on its own?

No single check is. Sound analysis catches things a walk-around can't hear yet, like early bearing wear or belt noise, but it can't tell you about accident history, odometer tampering, or flood damage. Use it alongside the document checks and the walk-around, not instead of them.

What if the seller won't let me test drive the car?

Treat it as a serious warning sign. A seller confident in the car's condition has little reason to refuse a short test drive with you in the passenger seat or behind the wheel under supervision. If they refuse entirely, it's reasonable to walk away.

How do I check if a used car has been in an accident?

Check panel gaps for unevenness, run a hand along body lines feeling for filler or ripples, look for paint that doesn't quite match between adjacent panels in direct sunlight, and check the engine bay for bent brackets or replaced bolts with fresh paint overspray. A vehicle history or VIN check adds a documented record on top of what you can see.

Does a clean service history guarantee there are no hidden problems?

No. A service book shows what was done, not what's about to fail. Regularly serviced cars still develop wear items like bearings, belts, and bushings on a schedule that a service stamp doesn't capture.

What documents should I ask for before I pay a deposit?

The Mulkiya (registration card) with a chassis number that matches the car, the most recent RTA or Ministry-mandated inspection certificate if the vehicle is old enough to require one, service records, and confirmation the vehicle is fully owned or the loan is cleared before transfer.

Can I use the sound check during the viewing, or do I need to record it and check later?

It runs in the browser on your phone at the viewing itself. Record a short cold-start clip and a short clip during the test drive, and you'll have a result before you leave.

Does Gulf heat affect what a used car needs soon?

Yes. Cars that have spent their life in sustained high heat put more strain on the AC compressor, rubber belts and hoses, and the battery than the same car in a milder climate, regardless of mileage. It's worth asking specifically about these on a Gulf-registered car.
