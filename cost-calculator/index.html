Costs Nobody Explains Upfront

  • Diminished value is a separate claim. Even a perfect repair can leave a car worth less on paper simply for having an accident on its history — some insurers will pay this out, but usually only if you ask.
  • "Betterment" quietly shrinks payouts. Insurers sometimes deduct a percentage for new-for-old on items like tires or batteries, so the check can land smaller than the estimate implied.
  • R&I labor pads the invoice. "Remove and install" charges for parts that had to come off just to reach the damaged one are normal, but almost nobody explains them before the bill arrives.
  • Shop supply fees are often a flat percentage. A 3–8% "shop supplies" line can get added to the total regardless of how much material the repair actually used.
  • Nothing locks you into the first quote. A second estimate is free and can reveal a surprisingly wide spread for the identical repair.

Why Repair Costs Differ: US vs Canada vs UK vs EU

A repair cost estimator built on U.S. data is a reasonable starting point almost anywhere, but it's not a like-for-like match once you cross a border. Labor rates in parts of the UK and Western Europe often run higher than U.S. shop averages, and UK/EU invoices typically add 19–20% VAT on top of parts and labor, which the U.S. ranges above don't include at all. Canadian shop rates tend to sit closer to U.S. levels but are billed in CAD, so the headline number looks larger without actually being more expensive. None of that makes an online car damage estimate useless outside the US — it just means the number is a baseline to compare against a local quote, not a substitute for one.

Rental Returns: Scan Before You Hand Back the Keys

Automated damage scanning at rental return isn't just a Hertz story anymore. Sixt runs its own AI-powered arches — branded Car Gate at some locations — across a number of US and European airports, and Europcar has partnered with vehicle-inspection tech from Tchek to automate its own check-in and check-out process. The pattern renters keep reporting is the same regardless of brand: a scan flags something after you've already left, and by then it's your word against a photo you can't fully interpret.

  • Photograph the car yourself, every time. Wide shots of all four sides plus close-ups of wheels and bumpers at both pickup and drop-off, time-stamped if your phone supports it.
  • Run anything questionable through a damage calculator before you dispute it. A rough independent cost range helps you judge whether a charge is even plausible.
  • Ask what the scanner actually flagged. "Damage detected" isn't an explanation — you're entitled to see the specific images or area the system marked.
