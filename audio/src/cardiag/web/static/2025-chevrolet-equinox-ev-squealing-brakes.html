Common Causes of Squealing in a 2025 Chevrolet Equinox EV

As a very new model, the Equinox EV's brake squeal complaints are concentrated in the first year of ownership, with owners reporting the noise on nearly every stop and especially pronounced in reverse. Regenerative braking on EVs means the friction brakes are used less often, which can allow rust and glazing to build up on the pads and rotors between uses, contributing to noise even at very low mileage.

  • Reduced friction-brake use due to regenerative braking: Squeal appearing despite very low mileage, worse in reverse.
  • Rust film building up between infrequent friction-brake uses: Squeal on the first several stops after the car has sat.
  • Pad glazing from light, infrequent use: Persistent low-speed squeal not tied to visible pad wear.
  • Standard pad wear indicator (less common at this mileage): Progressive squeal that worsens over time.

FAQ: 2025 Chevrolet Equinox EV Squealing Noise

Why would a brand-new EV already have squealing brakes?

Because regenerative braking handles most day-to-day slowing down, the friction brakes on many EVs get used far less than on a gas vehicle, which can let rust and glazing build up on the pads and rotors and cause noise even at very low mileage.

Why is the squeal worse in reverse?

Reverse gear uses the friction brakes exclusively on most EVs since regenerative braking typically doesn't engage the same way, so any rust film or glazing is most audible when backing up.

Is a squealing brake a normal part of EV ownership?

It's a known characteristic risk of infrequent friction-brake use on EVs, but persistent, loud squealing on a nearly-new vehicle is still worth raising with the dealer rather than assuming it's unavoidable.

Should brake pad replacement be covered under warranty this early?

Owners have reported mixed answers from dealers on this — some warranties treat brake pads as a wear item excluded from coverage regardless of mileage, so it's worth clarifying the specific terms of your vehicle's warranty before paying out of pocket.
