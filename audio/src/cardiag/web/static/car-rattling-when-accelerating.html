Car Rattles When Accelerating? Here's Why — and What It Costs to Fix

Reviewed by Carithm AI Automotive Research Team against OEM service documentation and automotive repair references Updated July 28, 2026 5 min read

If your car rattles when accelerating and stays quiet at idle, that pattern is a real clue. Parts that only shake under load — exhaust hardware, engine and transmission mounts, heat shields, or a stressed catalytic converter — behave differently once RPM and torque climb than they do sitting still. Car rattling when accelerating is one of the most common noise complaints, and the exact cause usually comes down to how the rattle changes as you press the gas. Each Likely Cause, Explained

Usually minor

Loose or corroded heat shield

Heat shields are thin sheet metal wrapped around the exhaust to protect nearby components from heat. Their mounting clips corrode over time, and once loose, the shield vibrates against the exhaust pipe specifically as exhaust flow and RPM increase under acceleration. This is the single most common reason a car rattles when accelerating.

Worth checking soon

Worn engine or transmission mount

Mounts hold the engine and transmission in place while absorbing vibration. As rubber mounts age and crack, the engine is allowed to shift slightly under torque, which can knock against the frame or nearby brackets. This produces a rattle or clunk that's most noticeable during acceleration and often worse when the transmission shifts.

Worth checking soon

Loose exhaust hanger or bracket

Rubber exhaust hangers keep the exhaust system suspended under the car. When a hanger tears or a bracket loosens, sections of the exhaust can sway and rattle against the underbody, particularly under load when the exhaust system flexes more.

Have it inspected

Failing catalytic converter

Inside a catalytic converter is a ceramic honeycomb structure that can crack or break apart with age or from contamination. Loose pieces rattle around inside the housing, and the noise typically gets louder under acceleration as exhaust flow increases. This is often paired with reduced power, a rotten-egg smell, or a check engine light, and is the most expensive cause on this list.

Usually minor

Loose accessory bracket

Brackets holding the A/C compressor, alternator, or power steering pump can loosen over time. These tend to rattle more at idle but can also buzz under acceleration as engine vibration changes with RPM.

Is It Safe to Keep Driving?

A rattle tied to acceleration deserves a bit more caution than a general rattle, since a few of its causes are more serious:

  • The rattle is paired with a noticeable loss of power
  • You notice a rotten-egg or sulfur smell from the exhaust
  • A check engine light comes on around the same time
  • You feel a clunk through the cabin when accelerating, not just a rattle
  • The rattle is getting louder day to day

If it's a light, steady metallic rattle with none of the above, it's usually fine to drive on short-term while you arrange an inspection. If any of these signs show up, get it checked before doing much more driving.

Questions About Rattling While Accelerating

Why does my car rattle when accelerating?

A car that rattles when accelerating usually has exhaust hardware, a heat shield, or a motor or transmission mount reacting to torque, or in some cases a catalytic converter with loose internal material. The rattle follows engine RPM because the part is being shaken by engine movement or exhaust vibration under load.

Is it bad if my car rattles when I accelerate?

Not always. A light rattle tied to a loose heat shield is usually low risk in the short term. A rattle paired with reduced power, a rotten-egg smell, or a check engine light is more likely tied to the catalytic converter and worth prompt attention.

Why does my car rattle when accelerating but not at idle?

Parts that move differently under load than at a steady idle, like exhaust hangers, heat shields, and engine or transmission mounts, will often stay quiet at idle and only rattle once torque and vibration increase during acceleration.

Can a loose heat shield cause rattling when accelerating?

Yes. Heat shields are thin metal and commonly loosen or corrode over time. Once loose, they can vibrate against the exhaust pipe specifically as RPM and exhaust flow increase under acceleration, producing a metallic rattle.

How much does it cost to fix a car that rattles when accelerating?

A loose heat shield typically runs $50 to $250 to repair or replace. Engine or transmission mounts run roughly $150 to $500 per mount. A failing catalytic converter is the most expensive common cause, typically $950 to $2,500.
