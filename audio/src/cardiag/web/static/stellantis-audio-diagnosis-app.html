The Truth About the Dealership Stellantis Audio Diagnosis Tool

Known Acoustic Failure Signatures Across Stellantis Vehicles

5.7L / 6.4L Hemi V8 3.6L Pentastar V6 ZF 8-Speed Torqueflite 3.0L EcoDiesel V6

1. The Infamous Hemi Tick (Lifter & Camshaft)

A fast, metallic tapping sound from the cylinder heads on Ram 1500, Dodge Charger, and Jeep Grand Cherokee. Caused by oil starvation to MDS needle-roller lifters, flattening the camshaft lobe.

2. Ram / Jeep Exhaust Manifold Bolt Snap

Loud ticking upon cold start that fades after 3–5 minutes. Caused by thermal warping on the rear manifold studs snapping off in the cylinder head, creating an exhaust pulse leak.

3. Pentastar 3.6L Rocker Arm Needle Clatter

Distinct clicking or clattering near the valve cover in Jeep Wrangler and Grand Cherokee. Caused by worn rocker arm pivot bearings scoring the intake/exhaust camshafts.

4. ZF 8HP 8-Speed Transmission Whine

High-pitched turbine whine that increases with road speed. Typically indicates internal fluid aeration, clogged transmission pan filter, or front planetary gear wear.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can this replace an in-person dealer diagnostic scan?

Carithm acts as pre-inspection decision support. It identifies the high-probability mechanical component by sound before you pay a dealer $180–$250 for a standard diagnosis fee.

How can I tell a cold-start manifold tick from internal engine damage?

If the ticking noise disappears after your Ram 1500 or Jeep reaches operating temperature, it is almost certainly a broken rear exhaust manifold bolt. If it gets louder as you rev the engine while warm, suspect a lifter or rocker arm.
