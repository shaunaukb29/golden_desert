Car Squealing Nonstop? Here's What That Usually Means

A squeal that comes and goes is usually just friction. A squeal that never stops — while the engine is running, at any speed, any temperature — more often means something is seized or failing outright. That's not the same as stranded. Here's how to tell what's going on.

Why Is The Squealing Constant?

A squeal that happens only during specific events (like turning or starting) implies a temporary loss of friction. A squeal that is constant, regardless of RPM, temperature, or speed, usually means something is physically seized or completely destroyed.

  • Seized Idler or Tensioner Pulley: The bearing has collapsed, and the metal pulley is scraping, or the belt is being dragged violently across a wheel that cannot turn.
  • Failing Alternator Bearing: The internal bearings of the alternator are disintegrating, creating a constant, harsh metal-on-metal squeal.
  • Water Pump Failure: The bearing holding the water pump impeller is failing. This often precedes an immediate coolant leak and severe overheating.

Questions People Ask About Constant Squealing

Is it dangerous to drive with a constant squealing noise?
It's generally more urgent than an occasional squeal, since nonstop squealing often means a bearing has seized or a belt is being destroyed rather than just slipping. A short trip is usually fine if none of the stop-driving warning signs are present, but don't put off getting it checked.
Why won't my car stop squealing?
Unlike squeals that only show up on cold starts or while turning, a noise that never stops usually points to a component that's physically seized or already failing — like a locked-up pulley bearing — rather than a temporary loss of friction.
Can a constant squeal mean my belt is about to break?
Yes, that's one of the more common outcomes if a seized pulley is dragging against the belt. If you notice the sound getting louder or more ragged, treat it as a sign to get it looked at soon.
How fast should I get a constant squeal checked?
Within a day or so is reasonable if there are no stop-driving warning signs. Get it checked immediately if the noise is paired with smoke, a burning smell, or a warning light.
