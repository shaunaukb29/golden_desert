Why Do My Brakes Squeal?

Most brake squeals are actually an intentional safety feature. Modern brake pads have tiny metal tabs called "wear indicators." When your brake pad material wears down to a critical level, this metal tab rubs against the spinning brake rotor, emitting a loud, high-pitched squeal to warn you that replacement is needed.

If your pads are new, the squeal could be caused by:

  • Glazed Pads: Hard braking can overheat the pad, creating a smooth, glassy surface that squeaks.
  • Missing Hardware: Anti-rattle clips or shims prevent the pad from vibrating at high frequencies.
  • Morning Moisture: Surface rust builds up on rotors overnight and usually scrapes off after the first few stops.

Is it a squeal or a grind?

If the squealing has turned into a deep, metallic grinding sound, your brake pad material is entirely gone. Do not delay inspection. Upload your audio above to verify the severity.
