Engine Ticking After an Oil Change? Here's What's Usually Behind It

Reviewed by Carithm AI Automotive Research Team against OEM service documentation and automotive repair references Updated August 21, 2026 7 min read

A ticking noise that starts right after an oil change is one of the more stressful automotive noise complaints, because it naturally raises the question of whether something went wrong during the service. The good news is that most causes — an underfilled level, an unprimed filter, the wrong viscosity — are quick and inexpensive to check and correct. A smaller number of causes, like a leaking drain plug or filter, genuinely warrant stopping and checking before driving further. What Does the Ticking Sound Like?

Timing and visible symptoms matter more here than the exact sound — use these questions in order.

Did the tick start at the very next startup after the service, or develop over the following days?
Immediate, first startup — compare with normal re-priming, but confirm oil level first before assuming it's benign Developed over subsequent days — more concerning — check for a slow leak or an incorrect oil weight
Is the oil level on the dipstick correct, low, or overfilled?
Low — underfill or a leak — top up carefully and recheck promptly Correct — less likely a volume issue — consider filter priming or viscosity instead Overfilled — can cause foaming or pressure issues — needs to be drained down to the correct level
Do you see any oil spots, drips, or wet residue under the engine since the service?
Yes, visible leak — stop and check the drain plug and filter immediately — treat as urgent No visible leak — narrow toward viscosity or filter-priming causes instead
Does the oil pressure gauge or warning light show anything unusual?
Light on or gauge reading low — treat as urgent — stop driving and check before continuing Normal reading — lower urgency, but still worth resolving the underlying cause

Does It Happen in These Situations?

A couple of specific service-related situations worth checking against.

Get it checked promptly

Ticking plus visible oil spots, drips, or a burning oil smell since the service

This combination should be treated as urgent. A leak from a loose drain plug, an incorrectly seated filter, or an old O-ring left stuck to the engine block can drain oil quickly and lead to serious damage if driven on.

Worth checking soon

Ticking plus a dipstick reading noticeably below the full mark

An underfill is one of the more common post-service issues and is usually a quick fix — top up to the correct level with the right oil and recheck the noise once fully warmed up.

Lower concern

A brief tick only at the very first startup after the change, gone within a minute, oil level correct

This is generally consistent with lifters or an oil filter re-priming after being briefly drained during the service, and typically resolves itself without any action needed.

Other ticking patterns worth knowing

Ticking after switching oil brands or from conventional to synthetic

A switch in oil brand or type is rarely the direct cause of a new tick on its own, but it's worth confirming the correct viscosity was used, since a labeling mix-up during a switch is more common than an actual incompatibility.

Ticking after a DIY oil change specifically

DIY changes are more prone to an unprimed filter, an incorrect torque on the drain plug, or an old O-ring accidentally left stuck to the block — all worth double-checking specifically if you did the service yourself.

What Should I Check First?

Work through these in order before assuming the worst.

  1. Check the dipstick on level ground with the engine off for a few minutes — confirm the level is at or near full, not low or overfilled.
  2. Look under the car (once cool, or carefully) for any fresh oil drips near the drain plug or filter.
  3. Confirm which oil weight was actually used versus what's specified for your engine, with the shop or your own records.
  4. Check the oil pressure gauge or warning light behavior at idle and while driving.
  5. Smell for burning oil, which can indicate a drip landing on a hot exhaust component.
  6. If it was a DIY change, double-check the filter was hand-tightened and pre-filled with oil, and that the drain plug is torqued to spec.

Have a check-engine code? If a light is on, note the stored code(s) before you clear anything. Entering the code alongside your recording gives Carithm AI more context and a more specific analysis.

Can You Drive With This Noise?

Ticking after an oil change spans the full range from completely harmless to genuinely urgent, which makes checking the oil level and looking for a leak the two most important first steps before deciding whether to keep driving.

Generally lower concern

  • Oil level topped up and correct, tick fades within a minute at each startup
  • No visible leaks anywhere under the car
  • No oil pressure warning light
  • Noise not worsening since the first startup after the service

Stop driving and investigate promptly

  • Any visible oil leak since the service
  • Oil pressure warning light on
  • Dipstick reads low
  • Burning oil smell, or a tick that's getting worse rather than better

This is diagnostic guidance, not a safety guarantee — when in doubt, have it looked at rather than assuming it's fine.

Questions About Ticking After an Oil Change

Why does my car tick after an oil change?

The most common causes are a low oil level from an underfill, the wrong viscosity being used, or lifters and an oil filter briefly re-priming with oil after being drained — all of which are usually quick to check and correct.

Is it normal for an engine to tick briefly right after an oil change?

Yes, a brief tick at the very first startup after a service, gone within a minute, is common as the filter and lifters refill with fresh oil — as long as the oil level checks out correct and it doesn't return at later startups.

Can too little oil cause a ticking noise?

Yes. An underfill is one of the more common post-service issues and directly reduces the oil pressure and cushioning available to the valvetrain, which is a frequent cause of new ticking after a change.

Can the wrong oil viscosity cause engine ticking?

Yes. Using an oil weight thinner or thicker than the manufacturer's specification can change how quickly and effectively oil reaches and cushions certain components, sometimes producing a tick that wasn't there with the correct viscosity.

What should I do if my engine ticks and I see oil spots after a service?

Stop driving and check the drain plug and oil filter as soon as it's safe to do so — a leak from either point can drain oil quickly, and continuing to drive on a genuine leak risks serious engine damage.

How long after an oil change should ticking go away?

A normal re-priming tick typically clears within the first minute of the very next startup and shouldn't return at later startups. A tick that persists, returns, or grows louder points toward a level, viscosity, or leak issue instead.

Can a bad oil filter installation cause a ticking noise?

Yes. A filter installed dry (not pre-filled with oil) or with an old O-ring left stuck to the engine block causing a double-gasket leak can both lead to reduced oil pressure and a new ticking noise after a service.
