How the AI Identifies Different Car Noises

The analyzer groups incoming recordings into acoustic categories before matching them against known fault signatures. Here's what it's listening for in each one.

Engine knocking

The AI analyzes low-frequency metallic knocking patterns that typically occur under load and compares them against known engine knock signatures.

Brake squeal

High-frequency squealing is analyzed alongside harmonic patterns to distinguish worn brake pads from temporary surface rust.

Wheel bearing noise

The system looks for speed-dependent humming and vibration characteristics associated with worn bearings.

Transmission whining

Continuous whining that changes with RPM is compared against transmission and differential acoustic patterns.

Suspension clunks

Short impact sounds over bumps are analyzed to identify likely suspension components requiring inspection.

Belt squeal

The analyzer detects high-frequency belt slip sounds and distinguishes them from bearing or pulley noise.

How Accurate Is AI Car Sound Diagnosis?

Accuracy depends heavily on what the AI actually has to work with. A few factors matter most:

Recording quality. A clean, close-up recording of the noise gives the analyzer far more usable signal than a short clip captured from across the garage.

Microphone quality. Most modern smartphone microphones are sufficient, but low-quality or heavily compressed recordings (e.g. sent through some messaging apps) can strip out the frequencies the model relies on.

Background noise. Wind, traffic, and cabin chatter compete with the mechanical sound itself. The analyzer filters this out where possible, but heavy interference reduces confidence.

Confidence scores. Every result includes a confidence score rather than a flat yes/no answer, so you can see how strong the match is, not just what the top guess was.

Uncertainty. When the recording doesn't provide enough reliable information, the system says so directly instead of forcing a guess — that's a deliberate design choice, not a limitation being hidden.
