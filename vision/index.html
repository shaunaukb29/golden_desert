How to Identify Common Car Noises

Why is my car making a noise when I accelerate?

A high-pitched squeal is usually a worn serpentine belt. A whine that gets louder with speed points to the transmission or a wheel bearing. A clunk suggests worn suspension components. If you're not sure, upload the recording above.

Why does my car squeal when I brake?

This is often a worn brake pad wear indicator doing its job—warning you before the pad runs out. A grinding sound instead of a squeal means the pad is completely gone, which requires immediate attention.

Why is there a humming or whining noise at highway speed?

Speed-dependent humming that changes pitch with your speed (not engine RPM) usually points to a wheel bearing. Whining that tracks engine RPM instead is more often a transmission, differential, or power steering pump issue.

Why does my engine knock or tick at idle?

Light ticking at idle that fades once oil pressure builds is common and often related to the valve train. A deeper, heavier knock under load or acceleration needs urgent inspection, as it can signal serious engine wear.

Understanding Designed Life vs. Mechanical Failure

Not all noises mean immediate danger. For example, front and rear brake pads have different wear rates based on your drivetrain (AWD, FWD, or RWD) and vehicle type (SUV vs. sedan), which affect brake biases. Our diagnostic AI helps determine if the squeal or clunk you hear is simply a component reaching the end of its designed life health, or if it is a sudden mechanical failure that requires a tow.
