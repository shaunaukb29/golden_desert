Things Nobody Tells You About Car Repair Estimates

A few things the body shop probably won't volunteer unless you ask:

  • Cash price and insurance price aren't always the same number. Some shops quote differently depending on who's paying — it never hurts to ask what the out-of-pocket rate looks like.
  • The dent is rarely the expensive part — the paint is. Matching modern metallic or pearl paint often means blending into the panel next door, which is where most of the bill quietly comes from.
  • OEM vs. aftermarket parts can double the price of the "same" repair. A factory bumper and a compatible aftermarket one can differ by hundreds of dollars for an identical-looking fix.
  • "Totaled" doesn't always mean unsafe to drive. Insurers typically total a car once repair costs cross roughly 70–80% of its value — a financial call, not a mechanical one.
  • You don't have to use the shop that gave you the free estimate. Most states require a written estimate before work begins, and you're free to shop it around.
