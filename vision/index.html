Average Car Damage Repair Costs (2026 US Estimates)

Wondering how much it costs to fix a dented car door, a cracked windshield, or a fender bender before you upload a photo? Here's a general cost range for the most common visible damage types our AI detects. Actual auto body shop quotes vary by state, labor rate, and vehicle make.

  • Door ding or minor dent (paintless dent repair): $75–$250
  • Dent with cracked or chipped paint: $300–$700
  • Deep scratch or key mark repair: $150–$900 depending on panel size
  • Cracked bumper repair or replacement: $200–$1,500
  • Broken headlight or taillight replacement: $150–$800 including labor
  • Windshield crack or chip repair: $60–$400; full replacement $250–$1,000+
  • Flat tire or curbed rim repair: $20–$150 for a flat, $100–$500+ for a bent or curbed rim

If your estimated repair cost is close to or below your insurance deductible, it's often cheaper to pay out of pocket rather than file a claim, since a claim can raise your premium for several years. Can I use this for my auto insurance claim?

The assessment is intended as an informational visual aid to help you understand baseline costs. It is not a certified mechanic's or insurance adjuster's inspection.

Does it detect fender benders and bumper damage?

Yes, it detects visible collision damage including dents, deep scratches, cracked bumpers, and broken lights common in minor parking lot accidents and fender benders.

What photos work best for the car damage scanner?

Clear, well-lit images taken close to the damaged area during daylight work best for the AI car damage scanner.

How much does it cost to fix a dented car door?

A minor door dent typically runs $75 to $250 with paintless dent repair, while a dent that has cracked the paint can run $300 to $700 at a body shop. Upload a photo above for a specific estimate.

How can I check for hidden damage before buying a used car?

Photograph the body panels, bumpers, and glass in good light and run them through Carithm's AI damage scanner, then follow up with a mechanic's pre-purchase inspection for anything structural the AI can't see, like frame or sensor damage.
