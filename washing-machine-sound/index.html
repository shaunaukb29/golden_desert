Normal vs Abnormal

What Does a Normal Washing Machine Sound Like?

Before diagnosing a fault, it helps to know what "normal" actually sounds like. A healthy washing machine has three distinct acoustic phases:

  • Wash cycle — a low, steady motor hum, the sloshing of water, and occasional soft thumps as clothes tumble. Generally quiet.
  • Drain cycle — a louder, high-pitched buzz or whir from the drain pump. Normal, though it should stop when draining is complete.
  • Spin cycle — a rising whirr as the drum accelerates, peaking at a smooth rushing sound. Modern inverter-drive machines (LG, Samsung) are noticeably quieter than older brush-motor machines.

Anything that stands out sharply from this baseline — a new grinding, a rhythmic banging, a persistent squeal — warrants investigation.

Sound Reference Guide

Washing Machine Sounds and What They Mean

Use this reference to match the sound you're hearing to its most likely cause. Ordered from most to least commonly reported.

🔩
Grinding High Priority

A rough, metallic scraping or rumbling during spin — often described as "gravel in a blender." Gets progressively louder as the drum speeds up. In advanced cases it can sound like a jet engine.

Worn drum bearings (most common) Foreign object in drum gap Drum spider failure
💥
Banging / Thumping Common

Loud, rhythmic banging or heavy thumps during spin, often with the machine visibly shaking or "walking." Check for an uneven load first — it's the most frequent cause and costs nothing to fix.

Unbalanced load Worn shock absorbers Faulty suspension springs Drum out of position
📢
Squealing / Screeching Moderate

A high-pitched squeal or screech, often appearing at the start of spin as the drum accelerates. May fade at full speed, or persist throughout. On belt-driven machines this is the drive belt's signature complaint.

Worn or slipping drive belt Bearing wear (direct-drive) Motor pulley friction
〰️
Humming (No Spin) High Priority

The machine hums but the drum doesn't turn. The motor is energized but can't drive the drum. Distinct from normal motor hum — usually higher-pitched, strained, or static rather than smooth.

Worn carbon brushes Broken drive belt Motor fault Control board issue
€™
Rattling / Clanking Usually Minor

Metallic rattling or clinking during both wash and spin, often with brief clicking. The most common culprit is a loose object. Empty pockets before every wash — coins, keys, and bra underwires are the usual suspects.

Coins or keys in drum Bra underwire in drum gap Loose cabinet panel Loose drum paddle
🔊
Buzzing / Whining During Drain Moderate

A buzzing, straining, or loud whine specifically during the drain phase. Some drain pump noise is normal — it becomes a fault when unusually loud, strained, or the machine fails to drain fully.

Blocked drain pump filter Failing drain pump motor Partial drain hose blockage
🔔
Clicking / Ticking Investigate

Rhythmic clicking or ticking that may occur throughout any phase. Often intermittent. Can be harmless (a loose paddle or cabinet screw) or a sign of relay cycling or electrical faults.

Foreign object in pump Loose drum lifter/paddle Relay or timer fault Loose cabinet hardware
Brand-Specific Notes

Washing Machine Sounds by Brand

The same underlying faults appear across every brand, but each manufacturer's design affects what those faults sound like and what they cost to fix.

Brand Notable Acoustic Characteristics
Samsung Digital Inverter Drive machines run quietly; bearing failure produces a distinctive high-frequency whine layered over grinding. Front-loaders are prone to rear bearing wear after 5–7 years.
LG Direct Drive motors are among the quietest available — any notable grinding or humming during spin is out of character and worth investigating promptly. LG's sound-settings menu can mute end-of-cycle chimes (not fault-related).
Whirlpool / Maytag Direct-drive top-loaders have no belt — squealing on these machines often points to the agitator dogs or drive block rather than belt wear.
Bosch / Miele European front-loaders often use sealed bearings integral to the outer tub — replacement requires the full rear tub assembly, significantly raising repair costs.
GE / Hotpoint Older belt-driven models make a distinctive slap-squeal when the belt starts slipping. Generally easier and cheaper to service than sealed-drum European designs.

Getting a Good Recording

How to Record Your Washing Machine

Your phone's microphone captures more than enough acoustic detail. Getting the recording right is mostly about timing and position.

  1. 1

    Wait for the noisiest phase

    The spin cycle or drain phase usually produces the clearest fault signature. Start recording when you first hear the unusual sound.

  2. 2

    Position your phone correctly

    Hold the microphone roughly 1–2 feet from the machine. Pressing it against the cabinet can muffle the internal acoustic detail you need.

  3. 3

    Record 10–30 seconds

    The AI automatically finds the loudest, most acoustically rich segment, so you don't need to time it precisely. A full spin ramp-up is ideal.

  4. 4

    Minimize background noise

    Mute the TV, pause conversation, and keep the phone away from other running appliances. Cleaner signal gives a more precise result.

  5. 5

    Upload and read your result

    Drag the file into the analyzer above (or tap to select on mobile). WAV, MP3, OGG, and FLAC are all supported. A ranked diagnosis appears within seconds.

FAQ

Washing Machine Sound — FAQ

What is the normal sound of a washing machine during spin?

A healthy machine produces a smooth, rising whirr as the drum accelerates, peaking at a steady rushing sound. Modern inverter-drive machines (LG, Samsung) are much quieter than older brush-motor models. Any grinding, squealing, or banging layered on top of this baseline is worth investigating.

My Samsung washing machine is making a new sound — what should I check?

Samsung front-loaders most commonly develop rear drum bearing wear after 5–7 years, which produces a grinding or whining sound during spin. Loud banging or vibration usually indicates worn shock absorbers or an unbalanced load. Upload a recording to the AI analyzer above for a more specific result.

My LG washing machine has no sound — is that normal?

LG Direct Drive machines are engineered to run very quietly, so minimal noise is intended. However, if there is genuinely no operational hum or drum movement during a cycle that should have started, check for a door latch error code, child lock activation, or a control board fault.

Does a sump pump sound like a washing machine?

Yes — sump pumps and washing machine drain pumps both produce a similar high-pitched buzzing or whirring during operation. If you hear a pump sound when neither appliance is actively running, it may be the sump pump activating nearby. Verify which appliance is running before diagnosing.

Is there an app that can identify washing machine sounds?

Washerithm (the AI tool embedded on this page) is designed specifically for this — it analyzes a recording of your washing machine and identifies the likely fault based on the acoustic pattern. No app to install; it runs entirely in your browser.

Can I damage my machine by running it while it makes a grinding noise?

Yes. Grinding almost always indicates worn drum bearings. Continuing to run the machine accelerates bearing disintegration, which can eventually damage the drum shaft and outer tub — turning a bearing job into a full drum replacement. Avoid heavy loads until you have a diagnosis.

What does a washing machine sound like with bad bearings?

Bad bearings produce a deep, continuous rumbling or roaring that gets significantly louder as the drum speeds up during spin. Early stages sound like rolling gravel; advanced stages can be as loud as a jet engine on approach. The drum may also wobble noticeably when pushed by hand with the machine off.
