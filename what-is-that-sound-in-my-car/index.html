What Is That Sound in My Car?

A new car noise can be difficult to identify because several mechanical problems can produce similar sounds. A high-pitched squeal might come from a belt or pulley, while a repetitive ticking noise could have a completely different source.

Instead of trying to describe the noise with words alone, you can use an actual recording as an additional source of information. Carithm analyzes the sound and ranks possible mechanical causes based on the audio and other information you provide.

The result is intended as a diagnostic starting point — not a replacement for a professional inspection.

How to Record a Car Noise for Diagnosis

A useful recording can make it easier to investigate an unusual sound. You do not need professional recording equipment — a phone recording can provide useful acoustic information.

  1. Record in a relatively quiet location. Reduce wind, traffic and background noise when possible.
  2. Capture the noise while it is actually happening. If the sound only occurs during startup, acceleration, braking, steering or driving, try to capture that condition.
  3. Keep the phone secure. Do not place yourself or the recording device near belts, fans, pulleys or other moving components.
  4. Describe the symptom. Mention when the sound happens, whether it changes with RPM or speed, and whether any warning lights are present.

Before You Try to Identify the Noise

Think about when the sound occurs. Does it happen only when the engine is cold? Does it increase with engine RPM? Does it happen while turning, braking, accelerating or going over bumps?

These details can be just as important as the sound itself because they help narrow down which components could be involved.

Is That Car Noise Serious?

The sound alone cannot reliably determine whether a vehicle is safe to drive. Some noises may be relatively minor, while others can indicate a problem that should be inspected quickly.

If a noise is accompanied by a warning light, loss of power, overheating, smoke, fluid leakage, severe vibration or a sudden change in vehicle behavior, stop driving when it is safe to do so and seek professional assistance.

Carithm's analysis should be treated as a way to generate possible explanations and questions for further investigation, rather than a definitive mechanical diagnosis.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is that sound in my car?

The cause depends on when the sound occurs, how it changes with engine speed or vehicle movement, and what the sound actually sounds like. Recording the noise can provide useful information when investigating possible causes.

Can AI identify a car noise?

AI can analyze an audio recording and identify patterns that may be associated with different types of mechanical sounds. Carithm uses this approach to rank possible causes of unusual vehicle noises.

Can I diagnose a car problem from a recording?

An audio recording can help narrow down possible causes, but it cannot confirm a mechanical failure on its own. A physical inspection may still be required.

What should I record when my car makes a noise?

Try to capture the noise clearly and, when safe, include the condition under which it occurs — such as startup, idle, acceleration, braking, turning or driving over bumps.
