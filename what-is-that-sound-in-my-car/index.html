WHAT DOES YOUR CAR SOUND LIKE?

Strange vehicle noises can be described in many different ways. These are some of the most common types of sounds drivers notice.

Squealing

A high-pitched squeal or sharp chirping sound, sometimes associated with belts, pulleys or braking components.

Rattling

A loose, vibrating or metallic rattling sound that may change with engine speed or road conditions.

Clicking

Repetitive clicking or tapping that may change with engine speed, steering or vehicle movement.

Ticking

A rhythmic ticking or tapping sound that can sometimes become more noticeable as the engine runs.

Grinding

A harsh metallic or abrasive sound that should not be ignored, particularly when associated with braking or driving.

Humming or Whining

A continuous hum, drone or whine that may change with engine speed or vehicle speed.

Knocking

A deeper knocking, tapping or pounding sound that can sometimes indicate a problem requiring prompt inspection.

Clunking

A heavier impact-like noise that may appear over bumps, during acceleration, braking or changes in direction.

Hissing

A sharp or continuous air-like sound that may come from vacuum, intake, cooling or other systems.

HOW TO GET A BETTER RECORDING

A clean recording can make it easier to analyze a vehicle sound.

01

Find a safe location

Park the vehicle somewhere safe when possible. Never put yourself near moving belts, fans, traffic or other hazards just to obtain a recording.

02

Capture the noise clearly

Hold your phone steadily and record enough of the sound to capture when the noise starts, stops or changes.

03

Add useful context

If possible, include whether the sound happens while idling, accelerating, braking, turning or driving at a particular speed.

Important safety note

Do not attempt to record a vehicle sound if doing so would put you in danger. If your vehicle has smoke, overheating, severe vibration, major fluid leakage, loss of control, sudden loss of power or another potentially dangerous symptom, stop driving when safe and have the vehicle inspected.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

What is that sound in my car?

A strange car sound can come from many different systems, including the engine, belts, pulleys, brakes, suspension, exhaust, cooling system or drivetrain. Recording the sound can provide useful information about its characteristics.

Can I identify a car noise from a recording?

Yes. A recording can provide acoustic information that can be analyzed for patterns associated with possible vehicle noises. Carithm uses the recording to provide possible explanations rather than treating the result as a confirmed mechanical diagnosis.

What should I do if my car is making a strange noise?

If the sound is severe or is accompanied by warning lights, smoke, overheating, fluid leaks, major vibration, loss of power or unsafe vehicle behavior, stop driving when safe and have the vehicle inspected by a qualified mechanic.

Can AI diagnose car noises?

AI can analyze acoustic patterns and provide possible explanations for some vehicle sounds. It should be used as a diagnostic aid and not as a replacement for a physical inspection by a qualified mechanic.

What kinds of car noises can be analyzed?

Examples include squealing, clicking, ticking, rattling, grinding, humming, whining, knocking, clunking and hissing.

Do I need to know what the noise is called?

No. That is the point of starting with a recording. You can describe what you hear in your own words and let the analysis help identify possible noise categories and causes.

Is a car noise diagnosis from audio always accurate?

No. Audio analysis has limitations and cannot see physical components, inspect the vehicle or reproduce every condition that causes a noise. Use the result as a starting point for further investigation rather than a definitive diagnosis.
